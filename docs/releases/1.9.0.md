nspawn 1.9.0 completes the names of machines, images, networks and volumes
on TAB, for any user and without a password.

## Added

- **Name completion in bash, zsh and fish.** Besides commands and flags, TAB
  offers the names an argument takes, asked from the service as it goes:
  - the running machines after `stop`, `exec`, `kill`, `pause`, `unpause`,
    `top`, `shell` and `stats`;
  - the images that do not run after `start`;
  - every machine and image after `rm`, `inspect`, `logs`, `restart`,
    `update` and `images rm`;
  - the local images after `create` and `push`, and their references after
    `run`;
  - the networks after `--network` (with `host`, `none` and `veth`),
    `network rm` and `network inspect`, and the volumes after `volume rm`.

  Secrets are not completed. `cp`, `-v` and `--device` complete paths of the
  host.
- **`org.nspawn.Names`** at `/org/nspawn`: `Machines(b all)`, `Images()`,
  `References()`, `Networks()` and `Volumes()`, each a sorted list of names.
  It hands out the names alone and asks polkit nothing, as machined lists the
  names of its machines and images, so a completion works under `sudo` and
  for users polkit would ask for a password. Every other method still asks
  for `org.nspawn.inspect` or `org.nspawn.manage`.

## Changed

- **The completion scripts call `nspawn` back.** What `nspawn completions
  SHELL` prints, and the packages install, is a hook that runs
  `NSPAWN_COMPLETE=SHELL nspawn -- WORDS` at each TAB; the binary on PATH
  answers from its own command line and from `org.nspawn.Names`. A script
  written by hand from an earlier `nspawn completions` keeps completing
  commands and flags only; writing it again brings the names.

## Requirements

As for 1.8.0.
